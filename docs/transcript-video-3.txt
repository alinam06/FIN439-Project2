I wanted to see whether the portfolio's rejection was sensitive to costs, risk estimates, or weaker expected returns.
Changing the historical window affects estimated volatility, but it does not change the maximum expected return when the forward assumptions and constraints stay the same.
In this 35 percent bond-floor scenario, the base return ceiling is 6.35 percent.

These results support the same decision: reject the proposed allocation rather than accept an investment strategy that fails the client's objective.

I'd be comfortable testing the process on paper. That carries no trading authority.
To bring the allocation back to the committee, I'd first need defensible return assumptions or a separately approved set of investments that can reach seven percent net within the bond and reserve rules.
I'd also verify fund eligibility and issuer exposure, check that 90 percent can be redeemed within five business days, and ask the client what loss they can tolerate.
