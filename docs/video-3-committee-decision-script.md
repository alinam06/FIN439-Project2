## 0:15–1:05

> **SHOW ON SCREEN**  
> In Portfolio Builder → Builder view → Allocation, show Scenario and mandate → Custom scenario, Portfolio and objective, Mandate limits, and ETF recurring cost (bp). Confirm the inputs match the verified scenario before recording.

**SAY**

I'm asking the committee to reject this allocation and authorize zero dollars. Here's the case I'm reviewing. It's a five-million-dollar portfolio with 15 years to invest and a seven percent annual net target. I raised the bond floor to 35 percent for this custom case. The original floor was 25 percent. I'm keeping the 10 percent reserve floor and the 15 basis point annual fee. The eight percent company cap still applies where required, and I need evidence for the 90 percent five-day redemption rule.

## 1:05–1:55

> **SHOW ON SCREEN**  
> Return to Investment Overview and show the target, ceiling, constrained result and live authorization for the exact on-screen scenario. Then show the comparison in Portfolio Builder. Do not display restricted numerical outputs without permission.

**SAY**

Here's the number driving my decision. Even the highest modeled net return is 6.35 percent. That's 65 basis points below target. Once I set aside the minimum for bonds and reserves, there's only so much room left for VT, which has the highest assumed return. That explains why there are no constrained weights on the screen. Equal weight also misses the target. The 60/40 benchmark misses it and holds zero modeled reserve. I use both as comparisons.

## 1:55–2:55

> **SHOW ON SCREEN**  
> Open **Risk & Scenario Analysis → Risk and allocation comparison**. Then select **Stress testing** and show the 1 pp LQD/VT shock. Leave the 15 bp fee in place and describe the separate zero-fee calculation out loud.

**SAY**

I wanted to know whether this was mainly a fee problem or a risk-estimation problem. The 60-month window puts benchmark volatility higher than the 36-month window, but both give me the same return ceiling. If I remove the annual fee entirely, the ceiling only gets to 6.50 percent. If I cut the assumed returns for LQD and VT by one percentage point each, it falls to about 5.54 percent. These are separate checks. Together they tell me lower fees alone won't close the gap, and weaker credit and equity returns make it worse.

## 2:55–4:10

> **SHOW ON SCREEN**  
> Open Committee Decision → Review area → Decision summary. Point to the approval gates, monitoring, recommendation, and download button. Avoid exporting or showing private figures unless authorized.

**SAY**

I'd be comfortable testing the process on paper. That carries no trading authority. To bring the allocation back to the committee, I'd first need defensible return assumptions or a separately approved set of investments that can reach seven percent net within the bond and reserve rules. I'd also verify fund eligibility and issuer exposure, check that 90 percent can be redeemed within five business days, and ask the client what loss they can tolerate. Volatility tells me how much returns can vary. It doesn't tell me what loss the client can accept. Once those checks are clear, I'd bring the proposal back for a separate vote.
