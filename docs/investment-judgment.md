# Investment judgment evidence

BoilerPortfolio separates a modeled pass from a suitable investment. The optimizer searches the modeled feasible set. The committee policy evaluates the active proposal, evidence gaps and the requested action. The Return strategist, Risk manager and Implementation reviewer explain different parts of the same evidence using deterministic calculations. They neither vote nor override a veto.

| Question | Method and investment interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| What drives the proposal's return and risk? | Each sleeve contributes weight × annual expected return to gross return. Subtract the recurring investor cost once. The Euler contribution to volatility is weight × (covariance × weights) for that sleeve divided by portfolio volatility. These contributions retain their signs and sum to modeled annual volatility when volatility is positive. A negative contribution offsets risk. At zero volatility the attribution is undefined. The dashboard and simulator compare the drivers with capital weights. |
| Does the optimum change when inputs change? | Value the original weights with the stressed inputs, then separately solve for the constrained minimum-volatility candidate. Report the largest absolute weight movement, one-way turnover (half the sum of absolute changes), gross trading (the sum), dollar amounts at starting portfolio value, and one-time cost on gross trading if specified. Compare the old and new ceiling margins using each scenario's own target. Infeasibility leaves no successor to price. The ETF return-only stress supplies no transition fee assumption. |
| How much room is there before the target fails? | With costs as the only change, maximum recurring cost equals current recurring cost plus the held or modeled ceiling margin. With equity forecasts moving in parallel, each mix's break-even cut equals its net headroom divided by its equity weight. For reoptimization, maximize that ratio over modeled feasible vertices. If a zero-equity vertex already meets target, the equity cut does not eliminate all feasible mixes. Classroom equity includes global equity and the fictional stock. Reviewed ETF equity is VT. Bond and other forward forecasts, covariance, and the mandate stay fixed in this test. Negative cost thresholds imply infeasibility even at zero recurring cost. Negative equity cuts express the required uplift. |
| What does the mandate constrain? | Show actual exposure, the rule limit and signed slack separately from the investor’s net-return objective. Eligibility and five-day redemption evidence remain unverified. Annual loss tolerance is missing. A separate sensitivity tightens the reserve floor by one percentage point with all other modeled inputs fixed. The benchmark can explain the price of meeting the mandate even when its own weights fail the checks. |
| Is the credit input usable? | BAA10Y is a broad corporate credit spread versus 10-year government yields. A validated, available, fresh vintage with enough observations can be admitted for monitoring. Missing, stale or insufficient evidence is quarantined. A rejected source import stays out of the decision. An accepted file still requires uploader provenance review. The spread can move through either yield leg and does not measure a particular ETF's spread or establish a trading rule. The [dated FRED example](../outputs/signal-decision-evidence.md) supports a separate November 21 monitoring judgment. The public app quarantines its live model input when no authorized archive is loaded. |

## Worked classroom results

- The default constrained mix has **7.15% gross**, **7.00% net** after **15 bp** recurring cost, and **12.10%** modeled volatility. Global equity is **70.10% of capital**, contributes **5.61 percentage points** of expected gross return, and accounts for **11.10 percentage points**, or about **91.8%**, of modeled volatility. A diversified holding list still concentrates modeled risk.
- In the existing higher-cost scenario, the held mix falls to **6.90%**. Reoptimization restores the target by shifting toward the higher-forecast fictional stock, which rises by **4.07 percentage points**. One-way turnover is **4.07%** (**$203,653**), gross trading is **8.15%** (**$407,307**), and the transition estimate is **$1,018.27**. Volatility rises from **12.10%** to **12.58%** and ceiling margin falls from **11 bp** to **1 bp**. This remains an illustrative example. Under the existing equity deterioration scenario, the held mix misses the objective and the **5.61%** ceiling makes reoptimization infeasible.
- The held mix breaks even at **15 bp** recurring annual cost, while any feasible modeled mix can meet target up to **26 bp**. The held mix has roughly **0 bp** tolerance for a parallel equity forecast cut, compared with **14.7 bp** if reoptimized. These are one-input mathematical boundaries. Interacting shocks, estimation uncertainty and unverified rules can leave less room.
- The reserve and net return bind. The investment-grade bond floor has **1.98 percentage points** of slack and the fictional-company cap **5.07**. Raising the reserve floor hypothetically to **11%** reduces the net ceiling by **0.7 bp**. This measures an opportunity cost under the stated assumptions. The active mandate stays fixed.
- The return strategist sees a feasible but narrow path and a benchmark trade-off. Risk management sees concentrated exposure and an unresolved loss limit. Implementation sees unverified requirements. The decision is **ILLUSTRATIVE ONLY** for the classroom allocation and **REJECT** for the actual ETF universe at the original target. **Paper-test the evaluation process** is a separate recommendation. Both carry 0% live deployment. A feasible reviewed alternative is held pending robustness, suitability, implementation and explicit approval. Above the break-even thresholds, a modeled shortfall is grounds to stop and re-evaluate. A hard failure or infeasible target rejects the proposal even if another perspective is favorable. The private ETF experiment still rejects the original target.

The displayed one-point reserve challenge and parallel equity shock are analytical design choices for student review. Neither is an approved new restriction or an empirical confidence bound. No forecast stress is a predicted market outcome. This note describes ordinary worked app examples and is separate from the student's official AI-off record.
